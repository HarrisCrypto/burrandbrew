## Who this guide is for

The hardest coffee person on your list: the one who already owns the good dripper, the decent grinder, and a shelf of gear most cafés would envy. You cannot buy them another starter kit. What you can buy is the upgrade layer — the instruments that make their existing setup measurably better, the beautiful objects that make it slower on purpose, and, at the very end, the one splurge that ends all splurges.

Prices below were checked October 30, 2026, on the makers' and retailers' own sites. Amazon's price for the same item may differ — check the listing before buying.

### Acaia Lunar Espresso Scale — $250

![Illustration of a small espresso scale](/images/gift-guides/who-has-everything/scale.png)

*Illustration — not a photo of the exact model.*

The scale that lives on café drip trays. Sized to fit under a portafilter, water-resistant anodized aluminum, reads to 0.1 g with a flow-rate display and auto-tare modes, so the shot's weight and time land in one glance. For the espresso person who times everything, this is the instrument the pros use. **The honest limits: the 2 kg capacity and small footprint are espresso-first — for big pour-over batches, Acaia's Pearl is the right shape — and $250 is real money for a scale. It earns it on speed and build, not on weighing better in a straight line.**

### Pullman BigStep Tamper — from $159.99

![Illustration of a premium espresso tamper](/images/gift-guides/who-has-everything/tamper.png)

*Illustration — not a photo of the exact model.*

The tamper with a résumé: a patented 58.55 mm base machined to a tolerance of ±0.02 mm, sized to cover the full coffee bed in precision baskets so the edges extract instead of channeling. Australian-made, heavy in the hand, and the handle woods and finishes make it feel like the gift it is. **Fit matters more here than anywhere on this list: the BigStep base is machined for Pullman baskets, and Pullman does not guarantee the fit in other brands' baskets. If their machine is a 54 mm Breville, there is a separate Breville BigStep — buy that one instead. Price runs $159.99–$189.99 depending on the handle.**

## The grinder upgrade

### Fellow Ode Brew Grinder Gen 2 — $399.95

![Illustration of a compact electric brew grinder](/images/gift-guides/who-has-everything/ode-grinder.png)

*Illustration — not a photo of the exact model.*

The single most useful upgrade on this page. Café-size 64 mm flat burrs in a countertop footprint, 31 grind settings, an anti-static finish that keeps grounds in the catch cup instead of on the counter, and a knock-out dose cup that empties clean. It is the grinder that makes every brewer they already own taste better. **One warning in bold because it decides everything: the Ode is a brew grinder. It does not grind fine enough for espresso — Fellow says so plainly. For a pour-over, drip, French press, or cold brew drinker it is perfect; for an espresso-first household, skip to the tamper and scale above. An SSP-burr version exists at $599.95 for the clarity-obsessed.**

## Slow coffee, beautiful objects

### Yama Glass Cold Brew Tower, 6–8 Cup — $295

![Illustration of a glass cold brew tower](/images/gift-guides/who-has-everything/cold-brew-tower.png)

*Illustration — not a photo of the exact model.*

Cold brew as furniture. Three hand-blown glass chambers stacked in a wood frame: ice water up top, a spiral coil that meters the drip, grounds in the middle, and 32 oz of cold brew collecting below over hours. It is the slowest way to make coffee on this list and easily the best-looking. **The honest notes: it wants a permanent counter corner, a batch takes most of a day, and hand-blown glass plus a household with elbows is a risk you accept. The bamboo frame is $315, and the 25-cup version — 100 oz of tower — is $470 if their cold brew habit is truly serious.**

When you are ready to shop: [AFFILIATE:yama-cold-brew-tower]

![Illustration of a ceramic dripper on a porcelain coffee pot](/images/gift-guides/who-has-everything/ceramic-set.png)

*Illustration — not a photo of the exact model.*

From Hasami, Japan — a pottery town with roughly 400 years of porcelain behind it — a dripper and coffee pot made from a proprietary mix of porcelain and crushed Amakusa stone, with a matte, natural finish no two pieces share exactly. ## What not to buy

Do not buy this person a second cheap version of something they already own — a $40 gooseneck next to their good kettle is a drawer filler, and this list exists because the upgrade is the gift. Do not buy the Ode for an espresso drinker, however good it looks: it will not grind espresso, and a disappointed unboxing helps nobody. Do not buy tamper sizes from memory — 58 mm, 58.55 mm, and 54 mm are three different fits, and the wrong one is a paperweight. And do not make the EG-1 a surprise. At $4,450, with two burr sets and two finishes to choose between, it is a decision to make together — the rare gift where the conversation is part of the present.
